# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

Horus is a technical project about building trustworthy systems. Everyone participating in
it — reporting issues, contributing code, reviewing changes, or asking questions — deserves a
harassment-free experience regardless of age, body size, disability, ethnicity, sex
characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education,
socioeconomic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity
and orientation.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be direct about code, considerate about people.** Rigorous technical criticism is
  essential here — a security project that cannot say "this check is wrong" plainly is not
  doing its job. Aim that rigour at the work, never at the person who wrote it.
- **Assume competence.** Someone asking a basic question about the capability model is not
  wasting your time; they are the reader the documentation exists for.
- **Show your reasoning.** "This is wrong" is less useful than "this is wrong because a
  revoked capability's snapshot would still pass the check here".
- **Accept correction gracefully.** Being wrong in public is the normal cost of working in
  the open. Nobody is judged for it.
- **Give credit.** Cite prior work, acknowledge reviewers, and credit bug reporters.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination in any form.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention or advances.
- Personal or political attacks, insults, or derogatory comments.
- Publishing others' private information without explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberate bad-faith argument.
- Dismissing or belittling someone for their level of experience.
- Anything else a reasonable person would consider inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Security-specific expectations

- **Follow responsible disclosure.** Report exploitable vulnerabilities privately, as
  described in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). Do not post working exploits against unfixed
  issues in public.
- **Do not weaponise findings.** Finding a flaw in someone's security code is a contribution,
  not an occasion for mockery. This project publishes its own critical defects openly; treat
  others' with the same seriousness and the same lack of drama.
- **Do not overstate.** Claiming a security property that is not enforced is harmful here in
  a way it is not elsewhere, whether the claim is about your patch or about someone else's.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all project spaces — the repository, issues, pull
requests, discussions, and any project communication channel — and when an individual is
representing the project in public.

## Reporting

Report unacceptable behaviour to **horus@pharanyx.co.uk**. All reports will be reviewed and
investigated, and will remain confidential to the extent possible.

Because Horus is currently maintained by one person, a report concerning the maintainer has
no internal escalation path. In that case, please raise it through
[GitHub's own reporting process](https://docs.github.com/en/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam).
Stating this limitation plainly is better than implying an independence that does not exist.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other
contributions that violate this Code of Conduct, and may temporarily or permanently ban any
contributor for behaviour they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.
Enforcement decisions will be explained where doing so does not compromise a reporter's
privacy.
